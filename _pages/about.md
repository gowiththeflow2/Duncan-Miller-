My research focuses on cellular imaging and microscopy, with an emphasis on understanding the organization of highly dynamic proteins within the nuclear pore complex.

My undergraduate research investigated how environmental conditions, particularly nutrient availability, affected bacteriophage infection and biofilm formation in Pseudomonas. I used Pseudomonas chlororaphis as a nonpathogenic model for questions relevant to the biofilm-forming pathogen Pseudomonas aeruginosa. This work introduced me to microbial physiology, bacteriophage-host interactions, and the importance of controlling experimental conditions when interpreting biological results.

In my master's program, I am currently developing and comparing chemical-fixation protocols to determine how well they preserve intrinsically disordered FG-nucleoporins for future two- and three-dimensional PALM imaging. This work combines my background in molecular and cellular biology with my growing experience in super-resolution microscopy and examines how interactions between fixatives and cellular structures can influence the biological patterns observed in fixed cells.
